## 1. Half the pin is inert padding

The pin sweeps **two** shapes, `rollback_ondone` and `nested_invoke`. The second
one does nothing. In both our verify script and the library's own
`tests/test_round9_findings.py`, the `nested_invoke` chart **never exits state
`a`**, so it fires **exactly 2 calls at every limit 1–25** and never trips
`RunawayChainError` at all. It agrees across all three lanes because a constant
agrees with itself.

This does not change the FIXED verdict — the discriminating shape does the real
work and passes — but the test advertises two-shape coverage and delivers one.
Either give it a shape that scales with `maxIterations`, or drop it and say the
pin covers one shape.

## 2. The general claim is false on engine-work-only charts

The changelog says "all three lanes agree at limits 1–25, odd and even, on both
shapes." On an `always` + zero-delay `raise` cycle — no timers, no delayed
sends, so the #206 carve-out for caller-`tick()`-driven sends does not apply, and
every step is engine work the chain budget is *defined* to charge — **24 of 25
limits differ**:

| `maxIterations` | sync laps | async laps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 6 |
| 3 | 10 | 12 |
| 10 | 23 | 33 |
| 19 | 41 | 60 |

Both lanes trip with `RunawayChainError` at every limit, so nothing runs away —
but async grows ~`3·mi+3` and sync ~`2·mi+3`, so **the gap widens with the
limit**: at mi=25 the same chart is permitted roughly **47 % more work on one
engine than on the other**. The two engines charge the `always` settle step
differently relative to `raise`.

We found this via a livelock fuzz (12 engine-work-only cells out of 352 configs)
and it is deterministic, not jitter.

**Why we are bothering to file it:** the runaway budget is a *safety control*.
We size `maxIterations` against a worst-case work bound, and a cross-engine
equivalence guarantee is exactly the kind of thing a downstream reader will rely
on when doing that sizing. Either narrow the claim to the two shapes actually
measured, or make the settle charge symmetric. We have had to write "do not rely
on cross-engine budget equivalence as a safety property" into our own config
guidance, which is a shame given how close this is.
