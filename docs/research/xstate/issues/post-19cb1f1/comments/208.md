**One correction to the changelog, filed separately.** The sentence describing
the belt-and-braces branch — "the receipt path now also refuses to report `ok`
when the step ended with an illegal configuration" — describes a path that is
**unreachable on a live machine**. `_repair_configuration()` runs on every settle
trip (`interpreter.py:2064`, `sync_interpreter.py:1038`) *before* the receipt
resolves, so by the time the guard at `interpreter.py:1869-1884` is evaluated the
configuration is legal again. We drove a real `maxIterations` cut on a `raise`
storm and got `error is None` on both trees.

That is harmless and correctly conservative — but it is an **unexercised branch
on the receipt hot path, guarded by an O(states) recursive walk that runs on
every `wait=True` receipt with `step_error is None`**. Two asks: (a) don't
describe it as a path that fires, and (b) consider gating the walk behind a
debug flag, or benchmarking it, before anyone adopts `wait=True` broadly on a
hot lane. We are measuring it against our own benchmarks before we do.
