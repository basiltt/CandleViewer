## 3. #220 — the recursive unknown-key gate, on our own charts

This is the round-11 change with the largest blast radius for a config-driven
catalogue: at `c78ce99` the check walked the **root dict only**, so a typo
anywhere below the root built a clean machine with a silently-missing entry
action or transition. `de2da4e` recurses.

Per chart, four negative controls were planted into a deep copy of the
corrected JSON and the build re-run under `strict_config=True`:

| Level | Planted key | Result on all 5 charts |
|---|---|---|
| root | `maxIteration` | ✅ `InvalidConfigError`, `(did you mean 'maxIterations'?)` |
| nested state | `entyr` | ✅ refused, path-named (`recording.degraded: 'entyr'`) |
| transition body | `cnod` | ✅ refused (`recording.idle on['REASON_ADDED']: 'cnod'`) |
| invoke body | `scr` | ✅ refused (`recording.starting invoke[sub]: 'scr'`) |

B14 / B15 declare no `invoke`, recorded as n/a rather than a pass.

**Our five charts build clean under the new gate with zero edits and zero
warnings** — no key any catalogue chart carries is unknown at its level,
including `tags` on states (B11 `degraded`, B13 `budget_blocked`,
B14 `live` / `desynced`, B15 `margin_call`) and `reenter` on B14's
`SNAPSHOT_TIMEOUT` self-transition. So there is **no #220 false positive
against the catalogue and no our-contract defect exposed by it** — the

## 4. #219 — do any of our actions await their own `send(wait=True)`?

**Audit result: no. This is the one place round 11 could have broken us, and
it does not.** The B11–B15 action inventory contains no action specified as
an in-step waiting self-send. The deadline actions
(`schedule_linger_deadline`, `arm_pong_deadline`, `rearm_pong_deadline`,
`schedule_backoff_deadline`, `schedule_budget_recheck`) are *timer-arming*
actions — under #212/#213 they are `raise(delay=)` self-sends, which are
fire-and-forget and never awaited.

But the tempting wrong implementation is one line away: "re-arm the pong
deadline **and be sure it landed**" reads naturally as
`await i.send("PONG", wait=True)` inside `rearm_pong_deadline`. `d5_reentrant.py`
implements exactly that on the real B13 chart and confirms the new contract:

- **async lane:** the action raises `ReentrantWaitError` with an actionable
  message; the `PONG` send returns in 1 ms instead of hanging.
- **sync lane:** the same shape is refused for parity, surfaced through
  `on_action_error`.
- **rollback holds:** because the refusal arrives as an action error under
  `actionErrorPolicy: rollback`, B13 stays in `ws_conn.live`, `status ==
  running`, `chain_trips == 0` — the chart is not corrupted by a bad action.
- **the good shape still works:** handing the receipt out with
  `asyncio.ensure_future(i.send(..., wait=True))` and awaiting it after the
  step resolves normally and drives the transition
  (`live → backing_off` on the deferred `SOCKET_CLOSED`).

**NEEDS-WRAPPER (small):** our deadline helper should expose exactly the
second shape — `arm(event, delay)` fire-and-forget, plus an explicit
`arm_and_confirm()` that returns the future for the caller to await
*outside* the action — so the refused shape is not reachable from contract
code by accident.

## 5. #218 / #221 — the catalogue's deadline shapes

B11's linger deadline and B13's pong / backoff / budget-recheck deadlines are
all the same shape: a self-scheduled `raise(delay=)` re-armed on every beat.
Round 11 fixes two ways that shape leaked.

**#218 — handle release.** A 200-beat, 50 ms heartbeat under
`maxIterations: 5`:

| Engine | Beats run | Peak live clock handles | After cancel |
|---|---|---|---|
| async `Interpreter` | 200/200 | **1** | 0 |
| `SyncInterpreter` | 200/200 | **1** | 0 |

`chain_trips == 0` throughout (#212: arming a delay ends the step's chain), so
a permanently-armed OMS deadline neither trips the chain budget nor leaks a
handle per beat. At `c78ce99` this ledger grew by one dead handle per beat.

**#221 — compaction safety.** A 30 000 ms linger deadline armed, advanced
10 000 ms, snapshotted (v3, one `scheduled_sends` record with
`remaining_ms: 20000.0`), then put through **two** load→re-persist hops with
no `start()` in between — the shape of a journal-compaction or migration job:

- hop 1 and hop 2 both re-emit the record **verbatim**;
- starting the twice-compacted blob fires at the **remaining 20 000 ms**
  (still `lingering` at +19 s, `stopping` at +21 s), not the original 30 000;
- after firing, a re-persist carries **no** `scheduled_sends`, so replaying
  a compacted journal cannot duplicate a deadline.

This one matters for us beyond the library: our persistence plan compacts
session journals, and at `c78ce99` that job would have silently dropped every
armed linger and pong deadline it touched.

## 6. #222 — sticky chain trips on the B18 priority drive

Re-run of the B18 `send_priority` drive (a self-feeding `SPIN` chain, then 60
external priority `EXT` sends, then `KILL`):

- 60/60 `EXT` applied, 0 dropped; `KILL` preempts and reaches `prio.dead`
  (unchanged from `c78ce99`);
- **`chain_trips == 1`** and `last_chain_error` is still a `RunawayChainError`
  *after* all 60 benign events — at `c78ce99` `last_error` had been recomputed
  back to `None` and the discard was invisible;
- `on_chain_budget_exceeded` fired exactly once;
- `clear_chain_error()` clears the latch and leaves the counter at 1.

Both lanes identical. For our supervisor this is the difference between "the
OMS silently discarded work" and an alertable signal; **`chain_trips` /
`last_chain_error` should be what `CvErrorHooks` reads, not `last_error`.**

### Harness note (not a library finding)

`SimulatedClock.increment` returns a `_MustAwait` when called inside a running
event loop. A `SyncInterpreter` drive nested inside `asyncio.run()` therefore
advances no virtual time — the first cut of `d4_timers.py` reported `0/200`
beats until the sync lane was moved outside the loop. Worth pinning in our own
test helpers; the library behaviour is documented and correct.
