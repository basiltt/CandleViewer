## 3. `strictConfig: true` — the new mandatory gate (#216)

This is the one mandated item that could have exposed an **our-contract**
defect, and it does not.

**Static audit.** No chart of B11–B15 carries a top-level key outside
`KNOWN_MACHINE_KEYS`. All five use exactly `id`, `initial`, `states`,
`context`, `actionErrorPolicy`, `guardErrorPolicy`, `onUnhandled`,
`strict`, `strictTargets`, `spawnBlockingTimeout` — every one a read key.

**Dynamic proof, both spellings.** All five build clean with the in-config
`"strictConfig": true` *and* with the `create_machine(strict_config=True)`
kwarg (10/10), and the policies are genuinely plumbed, not defaulted:
`action_error_policy='rollback'`, `on_unhandled` matching each chart's own
`defer`/`error`, `spawn_blocking_timeout_ms == 5000.0` on all five.

**The gate actually bites.** For each of the five charts I injected the three
typos the OMS is most exposed to and confirmed `InvalidConfigError` with a
correct "did you mean" hint (15/15):

| injected | hint | consequence had it stayed silent |
|---|---|---|
| `actionErrorPolicyy` | `actionErrorPolicy` | rollback → `continue`: a half-applied action set is committed |
| `Strict` | `strict` | unknown events silently accepted |
| `onUnhandledEvent` | `onUnhandled` | `defer`/`error` → permissive default: order events silently dropped |

The `x-` namespace and `meta` are still accepted (5/5), so our own
annotations remain legal.

> **Verdict: no our-contract defect.** `strictConfig: true` can be made
> mandatory in the CandleViewer wrapper immediately, at zero cost to B11–B15.
> This retires the round-9 `NEEDS-WRAPPER` item — the library now does it.

## 4. #212 / #213 on our own deadlines — the new ground

Our charts model every deadline as an opaque named action
(`schedule_linger_deadline`, `schedule_backoff_deadline`, `arm_pong_deadline`,
`schedule_budget_recheck`, `request_snapshot`). On 0.8.1 the natural wrapper
realisation is a delayed self-raise — and round 10 changes what that *means*
twice. `h_d6_deadlines.py` rewrites the named action into
`{"type": "raise", "params": {"event": …, "delay": …, "id": …}}` on a deep
copy and drives it.

### 4.1 #212 — a re-arming deadline is a periodic process, not a runaway

The B13 reconnect loop (`backing_off` → `BACKOFF_DUE` → `connecting` → dial
fails → `backing_off` → re-arm) is the shape #206 would have killed. Under
the #212 rule, with `maxIterations` set to **5** and the dial failing every
time:

| period | ticks | fires | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 ms | 40 | **40** | none |
| 50 ms | 40 | **40** | none |

Forty laps under a limit of five, on both lanes, with no `RunawayChainError`.
Arming ends the step's chain; the firing is a clock event. **This is the
behaviour the OMS needs** — a feed that reconnects for hours must not trip a
chain budget — and it is exactly what #206 got wrong.

### 4.2 #213 — an armed deadline survives the snapshot with its remaining time

B11 `lingering` snapshotted 10 s into a 30 s linger deadline:

```json
{"kind": "event", "type": "LINGER_DUE", "payload": {},
 "remaining_ms": 20000.0, "send_id": "cv-linger"}
```

* layout `version == 3`; exactly one `scheduled_sends` record;
* the record holds **20 000 ms**, the *remaining* delay — a restore does not
  silently extend the deadline back to its full period;
* `start()` re-arms it: the restored machine is still `lingering` at +19 s and
  reaches `stopping` at +21 s — it fires once, on the remaining time;
* identical on B13 `backing_off` / `BACKOFF_DUE`, and on **both engines**.

This matters more than it looks. B11 `lingering` has exactly **one** timed
exit. On 0.8.0 a recorder snapshotted mid-linger restored permanently parked —
never stopping, never releasing its stream budget, and indistinguishable from
a healthy linger by configuration alone. That is the #213 shape landing
squarely on our chart, and it is now fixed.

Standalone proof: `repro/r10_b11_linger_deadline_snapshot.py`, 11/11 PASS,
exit 0, from neutral cwd `<home>`.

> **Wrapper constraint (new).** Delayed self-raises must carry a **stable
> `id`**. The `send_id` is what lands in `scheduled_sends` and what `cancel`
> addresses; an auto-generated id would make `cancel_linger` unable to
> disarm a restored deadline. Every deadline in the catalogue needs a
> declared id.

## 6. Findings

### LIBRARY

| id | severity | status |
|---|---|---|
| **R9-DOC-01** | 🟢 trivial, documentation only | **STILL OPEN at `c78ce99`** |

The CHANGELOG contradicts itself on the storm plateau: the #207 entry says the
cycle trips at `maxIterations + 2`, the #210 entry says the test asserts the
plateau is `maxIterations + 3`. The library's own pin
(`test_round9_findings.py`) asserts `1000 + 2`, and my measurement is `+2`
(14 at limit 12, 1002 at limit 1000) on both engines and both lanes. **`+2` is
the truth; the #210 sentence is wrong.** No behavioural impact — but the task
brief itself repeats the `+3` figure, so the wrong number is already
propagating into downstream planning.

*Previously `NEEDS-WRAPPER`, now closed by the library:* the unknown-top-level-
key gap is fixed by #216 (§3).

### OUR-CONTRACT

| id | severity | status |
|---|---|---|
| **C-04** — B16 elevation survives `LOGOUT` | 🔴 blocker | **STILL PRESENT** |
| **C-07b** — B18 kill switch bricked by a denied `RELEASE` | 🔴 blocker | **STILL PRESENT** |

Both re-verified at `c78ce99`; both repros exit 1.

**C-04.** `elevation.elevated` handles `REVOKE` but not `LOGOUT`. After a
clean logout the session is `auth.revoked` (final, terminal-tagged) while the
elevation region is still `elevated` and `clear_elevated` never ran
(`clear_elevated_ran: false`, `elevated_until_us: 1`). Any authorisation that
reads the `elevated` tag rather than auth liveness grants admin rights on a
logged-out session. Control-path `REVOKE` works correctly — it is specifically
the logout edge that is missing.

**C-07b.** With `onUnhandled: error` on the control path, a guard-denied
`RELEASE` (wrong operator) puts the interpreter into `status: error` with
`last_error: UnhandledEventError`. The *legitimate* operator's subsequent
`RELEASE` is then accepted by `send()` but does not take — final state
`kill_switch.engaged`, `released=0`. **A denied release permanently bricks the
kill switch.** This is the single most serious item on the group: the kill
switch is the last-resort control and one mistyped release wedges it.

Neither is a library defect. Both are fixable in our JSON: C-04 needs a
`LOGOUT` handler on `elevation.elevated` targeting `normal` with
`clear_elevated`; C-07b needs the denied branch to be an explicit
self-targeted no-op transition rather than falling through to the
`onUnhandled: error` sink.

### NEEDS-WRAPPER

| id | item |
|---|---|
| **W-10-01** | Delayed self-raises implementing catalogue deadlines must declare a **stable `send_id`** (§4.2), otherwise `cancel_linger` / `rearm_pong_deadline` cannot address a restored deadline. |
| **W-10-02** | Restore must use `from_snapshot(..., minimum_version=3)`. A v2 payload upcasts silently, and a v2 payload has **no** `scheduled_sends` — so a pre-round-10 snapshot restores with every deadline lost. Refuse it rather than restore a parked recorder. |

## 7. Verdict for this group

**B11–B15 are clear of library defects on `c78ce99`.** The last library-side
behavioural item on this group (silent config typos) closed in round 10, our
contracts pass the new `strictConfig` gate unmodified, and the two round-10
semantic reversals both land *favourably* on our charts — #212 makes our
reconnect/heartbeat deadlines legal, and #213 fixes a silent
permanently-parked-recorder hazard that our B11 linger design was squarely
exposed to.

Adoption on the **order/data path** (B11–B15) is unblocked. Adoption on the
**control path** remains blocked by C-04 and C-07b, both ours, both with
standing repros, neither touched since `3ed3099`. These two are now the
longest-lived open items in the register and should be fixed in the catalogue
JSON before Phase 3 touches B16/B18.
