## 3. LIBRARY defects

### LD-01 — a `guardErrorPolicy: "raise"` failure on an engine-driven transition is *unobservable* and strands the machine — HIGH

**Reproduced:** `r3_guard_raise.py`, `r4_guard_raise_sinks.py`.

§1.3b mandates `guardErrorPolicy: "raise"` on the stated ground that *"a crashed guard is NOT a denial"*. On 3ed3099 that holds **only for a `send(..., wait=True)` caller**, and only via `Receipt.error`:

| Path | Guard raises → observable where? |
|---|---|
| `send(ev, wait=True)` | `Receipt.error` is set, `changed=False`. **No exception at the call site.** `interpreter.error` stays `None`, `status` stays `running` |
| `send(ev)` fire-and-forget | `interpreter.last_error` / `last_transition_ok=False` only. **`interpreter.error is None`, `status == "running"`, no `on_event_dropped`, no `on_resolve_error`, no plugin hook fires.** A monitor watching the documented signals sees nothing |
| guard on an **`invoke` `onDone` branch** | **Nothing at all.** There is no caller, therefore no receipt |
| guard on an **`always`** during `start()` | `start()` raises — this one path is correct |

The `invoke` case is the dangerous one, and it lands squarely on B8:

```
B8, raising `exchange_reports_sl` (the guard on read_position_sl's onDone):
  stuck_at:           ['sl.verifying', 'watchdog.scanning']
  status:             'running'
  interpreter.error:  None
  after 4 further events (SCAN_DUE, SL_DEADLINE, TIGHTEN_SL, POSITION_OPENED):
  after_pokes:        ['sl.verifying', 'watchdog.scanning']   ← unchanged
  deferred_count:     3    ← SL_DEADLINE, TIGHTEN_SL, POSITION_OPENED all held
  pending_invocations: [PendingInvocation(sl.verifying, 'ver', 'read_position_sl')]
```

A position whose SL verification guard crashed sits in `verifying` **forever**, reporting `running` and `error=None`, silently swallowing its own `SL_DEADLINE` into the defer buffer — in the one machine configured `actionErrorPolicy: "fail"` *specifically* so a half-applied safety step halts. `guardErrorPolicy: "raise"` does not extend to the invoke-completion path, and `actionErrorPolicy: "fail"` does not cover guards.

The only truthful signal is `pending_invocations()` / `has_dormant_invocations` — and see LD-02 for why that does not help on a live machine.

**Consequence for adoption:** INV-B8-a's *positive* claim survives (nothing reaches `protected` without a confirmed SL — proven exhaustively). What fails is the **liveness** half: the machine can stop making progress toward `protected` *or* `naked` with no signal. `naked` is never entered, so **INV-3's P1 alert never fires**.

**Wrapper required — W-01.**

### LD-02 — `pending_invocations()` cannot distinguish "service running" from "guard crashed, nothing will ever complete" — MEDIUM

**Reproduced:** `c5_followups.py`, `r4_guard_raise_sinks.py`.

`pending_invocations()` is documented as *"invokes in the active configuration that have NO live service… Empty on a live machine"*. Confirmed: with `submit_child` genuinely in flight in B6, four consecutive readings give `[]`. That is by design.

But it means the LD-01 strand is **indistinguishable from healthy work** while the interpreter is live: B8 stuck in `verifying` after the `onDone` guard crashed reports `pending_invocations() == [PendingInvocation(...)]` — because the service *did* finish — whereas B6 with a service still running reports `[]`. So the list is non-empty for both "restored, dormant" and "completed but its guard crashed", and empty for "actually working". CV-C20 tells `cv.statechart.persistence` to *"reconcile every `pending_invocations()` entry on restore"*; that is sound for the restore path, but the same API is the only health signal available for LD-01 and it carries no timestamp, so "stuck" is not derivable from it.

**Wrapper required — W-01** (a staleness clock is the missing input).

### Not defects — claims re-verified as correct on 3ed3099

- **#102 / `SnapshotMidStepError`.** 31/31 crashpoints across five machines snapshotted cleanly at quiescence; **zero** spurious refusals. Probing from inside entry, exit and transition actions on both `get_snapshot()` and `get_persisted_snapshot()` also returned `ALLOWED` in every case — i.e. the guard is narrow enough that it never fired on any legitimate contract shape here. The #102 window is genuinely narrow; it is not a usability tax.
- **Deferral buffer is snapshot-durable.** A `RESOLVE` held in B10's `delivery_failed` survived snapshot → restore (`deferred_count` 1 → 1) and replayed correctly after `ACK`. This is the clause that makes §1.3b's "House rule A3 is retired" *correct in principle* — CD-01 is about the leftover scaffolding, not this.
- **`Receipt.deferred` (#84/#106).** Reads `True` for a genuinely held event and `False` for a real no-op, at the caller's `await` point. **This directly retires the §1.3b "CV-C06 clause is WITHDRAWN AS UNSOUND" prohibition** — see §5.
- **`send_priority` (#107 lane).** Pre-empts 80 queued events; the B18 kill-switch mechanism works.
- **Bounded inbox.** `QueueOverflowError` under `RAISE`, no silent drop.
- **`restart_services` / `restart_timers` / `from_snapshot(clock=)`.** All three present and behaving: `restart_services=False` restores B6 into `submitting_slice` **stranded** (correct and documented — `pending_invocations()` reports it); `restart_services=True` re-runs `submit_child` and settles to `armed`. `clock=` removed the need for the old `attach_clock` monkey-patch used in the persistence track.

## 4. OUR-CONTRACT defects

### CD-01 — the A3 `"*"` scaffolding is **live**, and it disables both `onUnhandled: "defer"` and `strict: true` — HIGH

**Reproduced:** `r1_star_vs_defer.py`, `r2_strict.py`, `c8_fix_catalogue.py`.

Catalogue §1.3b, E50-T09, states:

> *"Until `E50-T09` lands, read the scaffolding as **dead but harmless** — with `onUnhandled: "defer"` set, the runtime holds the event before any `"*"` handler is consulted, so the inline handler never fires."*

**Both halves of that sentence are false.** The `"*"` handler is consulted *first* — `onUnhandled` is by definition only reached when an event matches **nothing**, and `"*"` matches everything.

Direct A/B on B6 `submitting_slice`, same machine, same script, only the scaffolding differing:

| | as-catalogued | scaffolding stripped |
|---|---|---|
| `defer` stub action ran | **1×** | 0× |
| `deferred_count` while held | **0** | 1 |
| `on_unhandled_event` hook | **never fires** | `("USER_PAUSE", "deferred")` |
| `Receipt` | `changed=True, deferred=False` | `changed=False, deferred=True` |
| final state | **`twap.armed`** — event lost | `twap.paused` — event applied |

The `USER_PAUSE` was **consumed by a no-op internal transition** and the user's pause silently never happened. The receipt actively lies: `changed=True`.

Second, independent consequence — `"*"` makes `known_events` universal, so `strict: true` becomes a no-op:

| machine | `"*" in known_events` | `strict` rejects a typo? |
|---|---|---|
| B6/B7/B8/B9 as-catalogued | **True** | **No — every name accepted** |
| B10 as-catalogued (no scaffolding) | False | **Yes** |
| B6–B10 fixed | False | Yes |

As-catalogued, all of `SLICE_DUE_TYPO`, `after.party`, `done.invoke.NEVER`, `xstate.forged`, `___xstate_forged` were **ACCEPTED** on B6. So §1.3b's re-scoped CV-C15 (*"a typo inside `after.` is still silent under `strict`"*) understates the hole by a wide margin: on four of these five machines **nothing** is checked. B10 — the one machine without the scaffolding — is the control that isolates the cause.

**Scope.** 4 of 5 machines in this group; 6 offending sites. By inspection of §1.3b's own wording (*"still present inline in all twenty contracts below"*) this affects **essentially the whole catalogue**.

**Fix applied.** `c8_fix_catalogue.py` writes `<B>.fixed.machine.json`, removing exactly these keys and nothing else:

```
twap.submitting_slice        .on['*'] = {"actions": ["defer"]}
chase.repricing              .on['*'] = {"actions": ["defer"]}
position_protection.sl.amending .on['*'] = {"actions": ["defer"]}
rule_instance.evaluating     .on['*'] = {"actions": ["defer"]}
rule_instance.acting         .on['*'] = {"actions": ["defer"]}
(B10 alert — none)
```

Re-running the full suite on the fixed copies: **51 PASS / 4 FAIL → 54 PASS / 1 FAIL**, the three recovered checks being exactly B6-defer, B6-strict and B7-defer. Snapshot sweep re-run on the fixed copies: still 31/31 MATCH. Build re-run: 5/5 OK.

**Catalogue actions:**
1. **E50-T09 is not cosmetic — reclassify it as a correctness fix and land it before any conformance work.** Strip the `"*"` handler *and* the `defer` / `drain_deferred` stub actions from all twenty contracts.
2. **Correct the §1.3b paragraph.** "Dead but harmless" must become "live and harmful"; the stated mechanism ("the runtime holds the event before any `"*"` handler is consulted") is backwards.
3. **Add `CV-LINT-XS15`: no contract may declare an `on["*"]` handler.** This is the lint that makes `strict: true` mean anything. Note `drain_deferred` also survives on `chase.working` entry and `rule_instance.cooling_down`'s `COOLDOWN_DUE` — harmless (a no-op action, not a handler) but should go with the same edit.

## 5. Needs wrapper

### W-01 — `cv.statechart.watchdog`: an external liveness clock per interpreter (closes LD-01 + LD-02)

The library exposes **no** timestamped progress signal, and `status`/`error` are both documented as not-liveness-signals after a restore. B8 can therefore stop dead in `verifying` with every documented health field reading green.

```
cv.statechart.watchdog.StallDetector
  attach(interpreter, machine_id, *, invoke_budget_ms: dict[str, int])
    # per invoke `id`: the wall-clock budget for that invoke to complete
    # AND for its onDone/onError branch to resolve into a new configuration.

  Mechanism (all from public API, no library patch):
    - a PluginBase recording (monotonic_ns, frozenset(current_state_ids))
      on every on_transition;
    - an external MonotonicScheduler tick (the same one B6 already uses for
      slice deadlines) that, for each live interpreter, computes
      now - last_transition_ns and compares against the budget for any
      invoke `id` present in the active configuration;
    - on breach: raise a P1, and for an actionErrorPolicy="fail" machine
      force the machine into its declared fault state.

  Additionally, every plugin MUST implement on_resolve_error and record
  interpreter.last_error / last_transition_ok after EVERY send, including
  fire-and-forget: last_transition_ok=False is the only in-band signal a
  raising guard leaves behind on the non-wait path.
```

**Budgets for this group:** B8 `att`/`ver`/`am`/`fb` ← `sl_deadline_us` (§B8.8 flags that 24 §8.8 and ADR-0008 disagree, 2 s vs 3000 ms — **must be reconciled before this wrapper can be configured**); B6 `slice`/`sweep`/`rec`/`cx`; B7 `arm`/`amend`/`mkt`/`cx`; B9 `eval`/`act`; B10 `deliver`.

**This wrapper is a ship-blocker for B8.** Without it, INV-3's "no position naked longer than `sl_deadline_us` without a P1 alert" is unenforceable in the one failure mode where the machine never reaches `naked` at all.

### W-02 — `cv.statechart.guards`: total guards, with a typed abstention

LD-01 means a raising guard is near-invisible. The catalogue's own guard contract (§B6.5 etc.) already says *"total; returns `False` on any internal error and logs at `ERROR` (A6)"* — i.e. **the guards were always specified as total**, and `guardErrorPolicy: "raise"` is the belt-and-braces. Make the belt real:

```
@cv_guard(polarity="deny"|"allow", name=...)
  - wraps the predicate; catches EVERYTHING;
  - on exception: log ERROR, increment cv_guard_error_total{guard},
    write context["_fault"], and return the polarity default
    (deny-polarity -> False);
  - a guard that has raised marks the machine degraded so W-01 escalates
    even if the configuration keeps moving.

Deny-polarity is MANDATORY on: tightens_only, explicit_audited_override,
exchange_reports_sl, sl_observed, attach_attempts_left (B8);
promotion_gate_satisfied_and_permitted, rearm_permitted_and_elevated (B9).
```

With `@cv_guard` in place, `guardErrorPolicy: "raise"` becomes unreachable-by-construction rather than load-bearing — which is the only way to be safe given LD-01. **Keep `guardErrorPolicy: "raise"` in the policy block anyway**, as a tripwire for an unwrapped guard slipping in.

### W-03 — `cv.statechart.invariants`: runtime assertion of the counting invariants

Several invariants are arithmetic over context that no statechart can express: INV-B6-a (`slices_done <= slices_total`, and submitted + shortfall == total at completion), INV-B7-a (`repricings <= max_repricings`, cumulative movement `<= max_chase_ticks`), INV-B10-a (`delivery_attempts <= max_delivery_attempts`), INV-B10-b (`delivered_channels ⊆ channels`, with equality iff `delivered`). The harness verified the *wiring* (each counter is bumped exactly once on the right edge, and the bound-guard routes to a visible state) — not the arithmetic.

```
cv.statechart.invariants.check(machine_id, context, state_ids) -> list[Violation]
  - registered per machine id; run from an on_transition plugin hook;
  - a violation is a P1 and forces the machine to its fault state;
  - the same predicates are reused as Hypothesis properties in E50-T03.
```

Cheap, and it is the only thing standing between "the guard was declared" and "the bound actually held".

## 6. Recommended amendments to §1.3b

1. **CD-01 fix is mandatory and blocking** — strip `on["*"]` from all twenty contracts; rewrite the "dead but harmless" paragraph; add `CV-LINT-XS15`.
2. **The CV-C06 prohibition can be LIFTED.** §1.3b currently says: *"a `send(wait=True)` receipt reading `changed=False, error=None` is **inadmissible**… **No order-path statechart ships** until either the library's receipt learns the defer disposition, or `cv.statechart` obtains an out-of-band discriminator."* **The library's receipt has learned the defer disposition.** On 3ed3099, `Receipt.deferred` reads `True` for a held event and `False` for a genuine no-op, at the caller's `await` point — verified on B6, B7 and B10, and it round-trips a snapshot. The stated precondition is met; re-purposed CV-LINT-XS9 should be retired and the ship-block lifted. *(Note this only became observable once CD-01 was fixed — with the scaffolding in place the receipt reports `changed=True, deferred=False` for a swallowed event, which is worse than inconclusive.)*
3. **Add CV-C23 (W-01):** every interpreter is constructed with a `StallDetector` and a per-invoke budget; no machine with an `invoke` may be registered without one. Reconcile the `sl_deadline_us` disagreement (24 §8.8 vs ADR-0008) as a precondition.
4. **Add CV-C24 (W-02):** every guard is `@cv_guard`-wrapped with an explicit polarity. `guardErrorPolicy: "raise"` stays in the block as a tripwire.
5. **`strictTargets: true` needs no change** — all absolute `#id.path` targets in these five contracts resolved correctly under it, with no sibling-fallback surprises.

## 8. Verdict for this group

**The library is fit for B6, B7, B9 and B10 as written**, once CD-01 is fixed in the catalogue. Every lifecycle invariant in those four machines is enforced by the engine as specified, snapshot/restore is trace-exact at every macrostep boundary, the defer buffer is durable, the priority lane pre-empts, and the bounded inbox raises.

**B8 is conditionally fit and is the only ship-blocker.** Its *safety* invariant is strong — `protected` was proven unreachable without a confirmed SL across an exhaustive 32-run guard × service × event sweep. Its *liveness* invariant is not: LD-01 lets a crashed guard on an `invoke` completion strand the SL region silently and permanently, with `naked` never entered and INV-3's P1 alert never fired. **B8 must not ship without W-01 and W-02.**

The most valuable finding is not in the library at all. CD-01 had two of the four Blockers that §1.3b's policy block exists to close — `onUnhandled` and `strict` — switched off on four of the five machines examined, by a paragraph that told reviewers not to look.
